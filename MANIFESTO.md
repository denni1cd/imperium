# Imperial Council Manifesto

## Purpose

The **Imperial Council** exists to **improve strategic decision-making through meaningful disagreement and documented debate**.

Its purpose is to help a person confronted with an important decision arrive at a **better strategy and a concrete plan of action** than they would receive from a single capable AI adviser.

That is the standard against which the Imperial Council should be judged.

---

## The Central Hypothesis

A single capable AI can reason well, but it tends toward one interpretation of a problem at a time and can converge too readily on a plausible answer.

The Imperial Council tests a simple hypothesis:

> **Strategic decisions improve when several independently reasoning agents with genuinely different priorities examine the same problem, challenge one another's conclusions, revise their positions when warranted, and have their disagreement resolved into an actionable strategy.**

If this process does not materially improve decisions over a single strong adviser, the Imperial Council has failed regardless of how sophisticated its architecture becomes.

---

## The Sovereign Convenes the Council

The Imperial Council is deliberately expensive in attention, time, and tokens.

At this stage of the project, **the Sovereign explicitly decides when the Council is convened**. The system should not automatically decide that a request deserves Council deliberation.

The Council exists when the Sovereign calls it.

This is intentional. The Council should be used carefully for decisions that merit deeper strategic treatment rather than becoming the default path for ordinary questions.

---

## The Council

The Imperial Council is composed of a small number of strategic advisers.

Council members are not just characters performing different styles of dialogue.

They represent **persistent differences in what they value**.

Each member has a strategic personality defined by a value matrix. The matrix determines what that member naturally notices, prioritizes, protects, risks, sacrifices, questions, and recommends.

Values are expressed from `0.0` to `1.0` and together sum to `1.0`.

A member's personality may affect how they communicate, but personality exists to reinforce cognitive identity—not replace it.

A useful council member should sometimes reach a different conclusion from another member because their values cause them to evaluate the same tradeoff differently.

Truthfulness, willingness to update in response to evidence, obedience to the Sovereign's stated objective, and intellectual honesty are not optional strategic values. They are requirements shared by every member.

---

## Independence Before Debate

Every council member must first interpret the Sovereign's original request independently.

No member should see another member's interpretation, proposal, or recommendation until all participating members have formed their own initial position.

Each member receives the Sovereign's original request, the same shared factual context, and their own persistent profile.

This prevents early answers from anchoring the Council and preserves genuine cognitive diversity.

The Seneschal may provide necessary shared context but must not tell council members how to interpret the problem.

---

## Full Disclosure Before Debate

Once all independent proposals are complete, the information boundary changes.

**Every participating council member receives every other member's complete proposal and the shared context.**

The Seneschal must not reduce those proposals to summaries, selected claims, or a pre-approved list of disputes before the members see them.

Council members must be able to understand another member's argument in full, compare it with their own reasoning, and decide for themselves what deserves challenge, support, defense, refinement, or synthesis.

After the Council chamber opens, all participating members should have access to the complete evolving deliberative record.

---

## Debate Must Be Real

Independent recommendations alone are not a council.

They are a panel.

The Imperial Council requires consequential debate.

Council members must be able to challenge specific:

- assumptions;
- interpretations;
- evidence;
- risks;
- tradeoffs;
- predictions;
- proposed strategies.

They must also be able to:

- support another member's argument;
- defend a member with whom they disagree on other issues;
- strengthen a proposal they did not originate;
- combine compatible parts of competing strategies;
- form temporary coalitions around particular arguments;
- and identify disagreements shared across multiple proposals.

A challenge must matter.

It should result in some combination of:

- defense;
- clarification;
- new evidence;
- refinement;
- concession;
- withdrawal;
- combination of competing ideas;
- or revision of a strategy.

Members are required to defend positions they still believe are correct, but may and should change their minds when presented with stronger evidence or argument.

They are required to pursue the strongest strategy consistent with their values—not preserve their initial answer for appearances.

Changing one's mind in response to a stronger argument is evidence that the Council is working.

Agreement that contributes no new reasoning or strategic contribution is not useful debate.

The Seneschal must not decide the debate agenda in advance. The councillors own the reasoning and determine which arguments deserve engagement.

---

## Debate Exists to Reach a Decision

The Imperial Council is not a debating society.

Debate exists to improve the strategy and produce a better plan.

Consensus is desirable when earned, but **consensus is not required**.

A debate should continue only while a council member has a material contribution that has not yet been adequately considered. Remaining unconvinced is not, by itself, a reason to continue discussion.

A deliberation should normally end through one of three paths:

1. **Convergence** — debate produces a strategy the Council broadly accepts.
2. **Synthesis** — debate produces a stronger hybrid strategy that supersedes the original proposals.
3. **Decision by vote** — material disagreement remains and further debate is no longer improving the decision.

All three paths must lead to one definitive Council result.

---

## Call for Vote

Any participating councillor may **call for a vote** when they believe the relevant arguments have been heard and further discussion is unlikely to materially improve the available strategies.

A call for vote does not silence unfinished reasoning.

When a vote is called, each member must be given an opportunity to identify specific unfinished business: a new material argument, new evidence, an unanswered objection, or a meaningful proposed modification that has not yet been adequately considered.

Simply repeating a previously stated preference or saying that one still disagrees is insufficient reason to continue debate.

If no member identifies material unfinished business, the call for vote is accepted and debate closes.

If a member identifies material unfinished business, the Council decides by simple majority whether another debate exchange is warranted. The Seneschal breaks a tie on this procedural question.

This mechanism exists to prevent both premature closure and endless repetition.

---

## Strategy Vote

When debate closes with unresolved strategic alternatives, the Council votes on the surviving strategies that emerged from the actual deliberation.

The Seneschal may clearly restate those surviving options, but must not invent a new strategy at voting time.

Each councillor votes for the strategy they now believe should become the Council's official recommendation. A member is not bound to their original proposal.

A strategy must receive a majority of participating councillor votes to become the Council decision.

If more than two strategies survive and no strategy receives a majority, lower-supported alternatives should be removed through a runoff until one strategy receives a majority or two final strategies remain.

If the final vote is tied, the Seneschal adjudicates the tie using the complete deliberative record, the Sovereign's stated goals and constraints, the evidence presented, and the tradeoffs established during debate.

The Seneschal must choose the strategy best supported by the deliberation. It must not manufacture a compromise merely to resolve a tie.

---

## The Seneschal

The Seneschal serves the Council but does not replace it.

The Seneschal:

- protects independent initial reasoning;
- opens full proposal visibility only after independence has been preserved;
- facilitates consequential debate;
- ensures material challenges receive responses;
- prevents circular or repetitive discussion from continuing indefinitely;
- tracks unresolved disagreement without defining the permissible debate agenda;
- administers calls for vote and voting;
- breaks procedural and final ties when required;
- adjudicates the deliberative record;
- and produces the final coherent Council plan.

The Seneschal must not pre-frame the problem for the Council.

It must not choose which disagreements councillors are allowed to pursue.

It must not manufacture consensus.

Its final judgment should explain not only **what the Imperial Council recommends**, but **why that strategy survived the debate** and what significant objections remain.

---

## The Council Must Produce One Coherent Plan

The final product of a Council session is not a collection of competing recommendations.

The Council must produce **one definitive strategic result and one coherent plan of action**.

After convergence, synthesis, or voting determines the Council's strategic direction, the Seneschal converts that result into a unified plan.

The final Council judgment should identify:

1. **The decision** — what the Council has decided should be done.
2. **The rationale** — why this strategy survived the deliberation.
3. **The action plan** — concrete, sequenced next steps.
4. **The important tradeoffs and assumptions** — what the Council is accepting or relying upon.
5. **Decision triggers** — what evidence or events should cause the strategy to change.
6. **Meaningful minority objections** — significant concerns that survived the decision process.

Minority objections are preserved because they may identify risks that later prove important.

They are not presented as competing official plans after the Council has made its decision.

The Sovereign should receive a Council recommendation, not be handed the Council's unresolved work.

---

## Final Plan Fidelity

Council members may review the Seneschal's final plan for one narrow purpose: to verify that it faithfully implements the strategy the Council actually decided.

A member may identify a concrete inconsistency between the decided strategy and the drafted plan.

A losing member may not reopen the strategic debate merely because they continue to prefer another option.

If a genuine inconsistency is identified, the Seneschal corrects the plan without reopening settled arguments unless the inconsistency exposes a genuinely new strategic problem.

---

## Action Is the Measure of Success

The Imperial Council is a strategic planning and deliberation system.

Its output should therefore lead somewhere.

A deliberation is incomplete until it identifies what should be done next and produces a plan that can be acted upon.

The Council should expose uncertainty rather than hide it behind artificial confidence.

Plans should be adaptable when reality contradicts their assumptions.

---

## Simplicity Is a Design Requirement

The Imperial Council must use the **simplest mechanism capable of producing genuine deliberation**.

Modern AI systems can interpret broad instructions, reason about workflows, delegate work, and coordinate complex tasks. The Council should exploit those capabilities rather than recreate them unnecessarily in software.

Therefore:

> **Instructions before infrastructure.**

> **Behavior before architecture.**

> **Evidence of failure before additional machinery.**

A workflow should not become a state machine merely because it can.

An interaction should not become a schema merely because it can.

A new service should not exist unless the existing system has demonstrated a concrete limitation that the service solves.

A feature is justified by improved deliberation—not architectural elegance.

If ChatGPT Work, a strong Council skill, a small set of persistent member profiles, and a Seneschal can reliably produce the desired behavior, then that is enough.

### Do Not Overfit the Council to Its Tests

Experiments exist to reveal weaknesses, not to accumulate permanent rules for every observed behavior.

Before adding a new protocol rule, role, component, or architectural mechanism in response to a test result, ask three questions:

1. **Observed failure** — did a concrete failure actually occur, rather than merely a result we disliked?
2. **Cross-domain relevance** — could the same failure plausibly weaken Council deliberation across different strategic domains, rather than only in the particular test case that exposed it?
3. **Minimal fix** — is the proposed change the smallest general intervention that addresses the failure without creating unnecessary machinery or constraining otherwise useful reasoning?

A change that fails these tests should normally remain an experiment-specific instruction, evaluation note, or open question rather than becoming permanent Council doctrine.

The Council must not be optimized to reproduce preferred answers from prior test cases. Domain-specific lessons should not silently become universal strategic rules.

Repeated evidence across different kinds of decisions is stronger justification for permanent changes than a single isolated failure.

---

## ChatGPT Work Is the Initial Operating Environment

The first implementation of the Imperial Council is intended to run **inside ChatGPT Work**, not as an API-driven orchestration service.

The project should therefore prefer native ChatGPT Work capabilities, Workspace Agents, Skills, files, and model delegation before building custom runtime infrastructure.

The initial implementation should be treated as a **ChatGPT Workspace Agent** whose deliberation behavior is defined by an **Imperial Council Skill** and supporting Council profiles and protocol files.

A plugin may later package and distribute those capabilities. An external app or MCP service should be introduced only if the Work-native implementation demonstrates a concrete limitation that requires one.

The repository is the durable source of truth for the Council's governing rules, skill instructions, profiles, evaluation cases, and accepted design decisions.

---

## More Agents Are Not Better Agents

Every additional participant consumes attention, tokens, time, and complexity.

Only agents whose perspectives are relevant to the decision should participate once the system has matured enough to vary membership safely.

The initial permanent Council should remain small and fixed while the core hypothesis is being tested.

New permanent members should be added only when repeated evaluation demonstrates that important decisions are being weakened because a necessary strategic perspective is consistently absent.

Specialist expertise may be consulted without granting every specialist a permanent seat on the Council.

---

## What the Imperial Council Is Not

The Imperial Council is not primarily:

- a roleplaying system;
- a digital company;
- an autonomous organization;
- a coding-agent swarm;
- a general-purpose workflow engine;
- a task-distribution framework;
- a simulation of bureaucracy;
- a mechanism for maximizing the number of agents involved in a problem;
- or an autonomous execution framework.

These are not requirements of the Imperial Council.

The Council exists to **think better before acting**.

Execution capabilities may emerge elsewhere later, but they must remain subordinate to strategic deliberation unless the Sovereign explicitly authorizes otherwise.

---

## The Sovereign

The Imperial Council advises.

The Sovereign decides.

The user's goals, constraints, values, and explicit instructions are authoritative.

Council members may challenge assumptions—including assumptions made by the Sovereign—when doing so could materially improve the decision.

They may not quietly substitute their own objective for the one they were asked to pursue.

The Council should make disagreement useful without making the system disobedient.

---

## Validation Before Expansion

The Imperial Council should earn additional complexity.

The first objective is to demonstrate that the Council works.

The minimum viable Imperial Council must prove that:

- council members form genuinely independent initial interpretations;
- their value matrices produce materially different reasoning;
- every member can read and understand every other member's complete proposal after the independent phase;
- members directly engage with one another's arguments;
- alliances, defenses, challenges, and hybrid strategies can emerge naturally rather than being assigned by the Seneschal;
- debate changes, refines, combines, or eliminates proposed strategies;
- the Council can terminate unproductive disagreement through a call for vote;
- voting produces a definitive strategic result when consensus does not emerge;
- meaningful dissent is preserved;
- the Seneschal adjudicates and synthesizes rather than merely summarizes;
- the result produces one coherent actionable plan;
- and the resulting decisions are measurably better than those produced by a single capable adviser.

The core comparison should include:

1. a single strategic adviser;
2. several independent advisers without debate;
3. the full Imperial Council deliberation process.

Until those claims are demonstrated, infrastructure beyond what is necessary to test them is premature.

Expansion should occur only in response to demonstrated needs or limitations.

---

## Governing Principle

When there is tension between making the Imperial Council **more elaborate** and making it **more useful**, choose useful.

When there is tension between performing a convincing debate and having a consequential one, choose consequential.

When there is tension between preserving an agent's character and accepting a better argument, choose the better argument.

When there is tension between continuing debate and making a decision after the relevant arguments have been exhausted, decide.

When there is tension between building another component and trusting a capable model with broader instructions, try the broader instruction first.

**The Imperial Council exists to produce better judgment, sharper strategy, and action—not machinery.**
